Privacy

HNx is a lightweight Hacker News reader built to stay out of your way — and out of your data.

What we collect

  • No accounts. There is nothing to sign up for.
  • No analytics or ads. No trackers, pixels, or marketing SDKs.
  • No third-party scripts in your browser. Fonts and libraries are self-hosted.
  • No persistent preferences. Theme and feed choices stay in memory for the session and are not written to cookies or local storage.

What your browser talks to

For story and comment data, the page only requests this site’s own origin (/api/*). There is no client-side call to Google Fonts, Firebase, Algolia, or other third-party APIs from the browser under normal use.

Our servers fetch public Hacker News data from the official HN Firebase API (stories, items, and progressive comment pages) so the browser does not need a direct relationship with upstream hosts.

What the host may see

Like any website, the hosting provider (Vercel) processes standard request metadata such as IP address, user agent, URL path, and timestamps for delivery and abuse prevention. HNx does not add product analytics on top of that.

That metadata is inherent to how the web works: the server that answers your request can always see the network path used to reach it. If you want to reduce how much of you is visible there, these steps help:

  • Use a trustworthy VPN. A good VPN replaces your home or mobile IP with the provider’s. The host then sees the VPN exit, not your ISP address. Prefer providers with a clear no-logs policy, audited claims, and jurisdiction you are comfortable with. Remember: you are shifting trust from your ISP (and this host’s view of your IP) to the VPN operator.
  • Tor Browser for stronger anonymity. Tor routes traffic through volunteer relays so the site typically sees a Tor exit node rather than your IP. It is slower and some sites block exits, but it is the strongest common option when you do not want your address tied to visits. Download only from the official Tor Project site.
  • Prefer a privacy-respecting browser and defaults. Firefox (with strict tracking protection), Brave, or similar tools reduce fingerprinting and cross-site tracking. Keep the browser updated. Avoid logging into personal accounts in the same profile you use for sensitive browsing if you care about unlinkability.
  • Use private DNS when you can. DNS-over-HTTPS or DNS-over-TLS (or your VPN’s DNS) stops casual observers on your local network from seeing every hostname you resolve. It does not hide visits from this site’s host once you connect.
  • Be careful with extensions and “free” proxies. Browser extensions can read the pages you visit. Only install ones you trust. Random free proxy sites often log more than they hide.
  • Separate contexts when it matters. A dedicated browser profile, container tabs, or a separate device for high-sensitivity reading limits how this visit links to your everyday identity.

None of these make you invisible, and HNx cannot force them on your behalf. They are tools you control. For deeper guidance, the EFF’s privacy tools overview and Surveillance Self-Defense guides are solid starting points.

What leaves when you open a story

Links to external articles open on the destination site. Those sites (and any trackers they embed) are outside HNx’s control. Outbound links use rel="noopener noreferrer" and a strict referrer policy so we do not leak this site’s path as a referrer.

Security measures

  • Content-Security-Policy locked to first-party assets and APIs
  • Strict-Transport-Security, frame denial, nosniff, Permissions-Policy
  • HTML from HN sanitized with a vendored DOMPurify build before render
  • No open URL-fetch proxy endpoints
