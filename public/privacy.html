Privacy

HNx is a lightweight Hacker News reader built to stay out of your way — and out of your data.

What we collect

Optional alerts store only your push endpoint, its delivery keys, and your rules as personal data, and “Delete all my alerts” removes them.

  • No accounts. There is nothing to sign up for.
  • No analytics or ads. No trackers, pixels, or marketing SDKs.
  • Self-hosted app code. HNx serves its own fonts and libraries. A live article preview can run the publisher’s scripts inside its frame.
  • Session-only theme. Light/dark mode is stored in sessionStorage for this tab only (so this page can match the app). It is not written to cookies or long-term local storage, and it clears when the tab is closed. The same storage remembers recent article preview decisions for up to a day. Feed choice stays in memory.
  • If you dismiss the iOS install hint, this browser remembers the dismissal in localStorage.

No JavaScript

If scripts are disabled, use the plain HTML reader (/plain) and a classic 90s skin (/classic). Both are server-rendered first-party HTML and CSS only — same privacy posture, no client-side JS required.

What your browser talks to

For story and comment data, the page only requests this site’s own origin (/api/* or plain HTML routes). The full app requests small publisher icons from Google’s favicon service using the publisher’s domain. Google can see your IP address and the requested domain. These requests send no referrer. The plain and classic readers omit these icons.

Our servers fetch public Hacker News data from the official HN Firebase API (stories, items, and progressive comment pages) so the browser does not need a direct relationship with upstream hosts.

Saved stories and offline reading

Saved bookmarks, article text, and downloaded images are stored in this browser. Remove a story or clear saved stories from the Saved tab. The app also stores its interface and recently loaded feed pages on this device for offline use.

When you save an article, HNx requests the public article and up to eight images from their publishers on your behalf. Those requests carry no browser cookies or authorization headers. Your browser sends the Hacker News story ID to HNx, which looks up the article URL.

What the host may see

Like any website, the hosting provider (Vercel) processes standard request metadata such as IP address, user agent, URL path, and timestamps for delivery and abuse prevention. HNx does not add product analytics on top of that.

That metadata is inherent to how the web works: the server that answers your request can always see the network path used to reach it. If you want to reduce how much of you is visible there, these steps help:

  • Use a trustworthy VPN. A good VPN replaces your home or mobile IP with the provider’s. The host then sees the VPN exit, not your ISP address. Prefer providers with a clear no-logs policy, audited claims, and jurisdiction you are comfortable with. Remember: you are shifting trust from your ISP (and this host’s view of your IP) to the VPN operator.
  • Tor Browser for stronger anonymity. Tor routes traffic through volunteer relays so the site typically sees a Tor exit node rather than your IP. It is slower and some sites block exits, but it is the strongest common option when you do not want your address tied to visits. Download only from the official Tor Project site.
  • Prefer a privacy-respecting browser and defaults. Firefox (with strict tracking protection), Brave, or similar tools reduce fingerprinting and cross-site tracking. Keep the browser updated. Avoid logging into personal accounts in the same profile you use for sensitive browsing if you care about unlinkability.
  • Use private DNS when you can. DNS-over-HTTPS or DNS-over-TLS (or your VPN’s DNS) stops casual observers on your local network from seeing every hostname you resolve. It does not hide visits from this site’s host once you connect.
  • Be careful with extensions and “free” proxies. Browser extensions can read the pages you visit. Only install ones you trust. Random free proxy sites often log more than they hide.
  • Separate contexts when it matters. A dedicated browser profile, container tabs, or a separate device for high-sensitivity reading limits how this visit links to your everyday identity.

None of these make you invisible, and HNx cannot force them on your behalf. They are tools you control. For deeper guidance, the EFF’s privacy tools overview and Surveillance Self-Defense guides are solid starting points.

What leaves when you open a story

Tapping a mobile headline sends its Hacker News story ID to HNx. The server looks up the URL and checks whether the publisher permits framing and can fetch a reader copy without sending your cookies or credentials. Successful reader copies can stay in server memory for an hour. The host briefly counts requests by IP to limit abuse.

A live preview connects your browser to the publisher and its embedded services. They can see your IP and may use cookies under your browser’s rules. Reader mode displays sanitized text and loads article images from their hosts. Preview frames, article images, and outbound links send no HNx referrer.

Security measures

  • Content-Security-Policy with default-src 'none' and first-party allowlists for scripts, styles, fonts, and API calls. The modern app also allows publisher frames, HTTPS article images, and locally saved image blobs.
  • Strict-Transport-Security, frame denial, nosniff, Permissions-Policy
  • Full app: HN HTML sanitized with vendored DOMPurify before render. Plain HTML reader: parser-based server-side allowlist sanitizer (http(s) links only).
  • Reader and preview requests resolve their destinations from Hacker News and enforce public network addresses, time limits, and response size limits.
